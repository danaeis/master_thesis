\begin{latin}

\begin{center}
\textbf{Abstract}
\end{center}
\baselineskip=.8\baselineskip

Automatic segmentation of abdominal organs on non-contrast CT, especially organs
with low contrast against surrounding tissue, is less accurate than on
contrast-enhanced phases, yet for many patients the non-contrast phase is the
only one available.

Improving this by directly training segmentation models is difficult, because
labelled non-contrast data are scarce and the sharp difference in intensity and
contrast between phases makes it hard to build precise, aligned pairs from
multiphase images. Moreover, conventional image-similarity metrics do not
necessarily indicate how useful a synthesized image is for segmentation.

This thesis proposes an approach based on synthesizing the contrast-enhanced
phase from the non-contrast image, to supply the information needed for better
organ separation without changing the segmentation model. To this end, a
multi-step rigid pre-processing and registration pipeline focused on structures
that do not move between the two scans was designed, and the resulting aligned pairs
were used to train PLC-Net. By conditioning on the target phase and enhancement
level, this network can generate different contrast-enhanced phases.

Evaluation on the VinDr-Multiphase dataset showed that the proposed
pre-processing significantly reduces the number of large registration errors
across all four methods examined. The 2D PLC-Net also achieved the lowest
organ-level HU error among the compared methods, and raised the average Dice of
the portal and splenic veins — only 0.55 without contrast, the lowest among the
organs studied — to about 0.72, consistently across all 20 test cases. Among
image-level metrics, only organ-level HU error was found to predict downstream
utility.

\bigskip\noindent\textbf{Keywords}:
Multi-organ segmentation, Abdominal CT, Non-contrast CT, Contrast-phase
detection, Inter-phase image registration, Medical image synthesis, Dataset bias

\end{latin}
